Clustering as Unsupervised Learning

No ground-truth labels — the model learns similarity/difference directly from the data.

Clustering groups unlabeled examples so a trained model can say "this new point belongs with that group" — contrast with classification, which learns from labels that already exist.

Use caseGoalInput features
Heartbeat morphologyGroup ECG heartbeat shapes for diagnosisRR interval, QRS width, waveform PCA, deep embeddings
Customer segmentationGroup customers for targeted marketingDemographics, spending score, purchase history
Social listeningGroup posts into themesTF-IDF/Word2Vec, timestamp, platform, engagement
Single-cell RNA-seqIdentify cell types from gene expressionCell × gene count matrix

K-Means

Fast and simple — but only as good as its starting point.

Distance metric (squared Euclidean): ‖x−c‖² = (x₁−c₁)² + (x₂−c₂)²

  1. Fix K (number of clusters) in advance.
  2. Pick K random points as initial centroids.
  3. Assign each point to its nearest centroid.
  4. Recompute each centroid as the mean of its assigned points.
  5. Repeat 3–4 until assignments stop changing or max iterations is hit.
Quiz

Is K-Means deterministic? No — random initial centroids mean different runs can converge to different results.

Local optima & inertia

Different random seeds → different final clusterings, some clearly worse than others (three runs in the deck scored WCSS of 444.9, 349.7, and 451.4 — nearly identical starting conditions, meaningfully different outcomes). Fix: run multiple times with different seeds, keep the run with the lowest inertia.

Inertia / WCSS (within-cluster sum of squares) — lower is more compactW_k = Σᵢ Σ_{x∈Cᵢ} ‖x − μᵢ‖²
3

Choosing K

K-Means needs K handed to it — two ways to estimate a reasonable value.

Elbow method

Plot total WCSS against candidate K values; pick the K where the curve's descent sharply flattens (the "elbow") — beyond that point, extra clusters buy very little.

Watch out

The elbow is a heuristic, not an optimum: the "right" elbow is subjective, it assumes roughly spherical equal-sized clusters, it's sensitive to the same initialization randomness as K-Means itself, and it's expensive to recompute across many K values on large data. ("There is no 'optimal' solution in cluster analysis — interestingness is a subjective decision of the user." — Schubert, 2022)

Gap statistic — a more principled alternative

  1. Compute log W(K) — the log within-cluster dispersion on the real data.
  2. Simulate B reference datasets drawn uniformly over the same bounding box; average their log Wunif(K).
  3. Gap(K) = (1/B)Σ log Wunif,b(K) − log W(K)
  4. Compute a standard error SE(K) across the simulations.
  5. Pick the smallest K such that Gap(K) ≥ Gap(K+1) − SE(K+1).

Intuition: the gap measures how much more compact the real clustering is than what pure chance would produce — a bigger gap means genuinely non-random structure, not just an artifact of adding clusters.

4

Where K-Means Breaks

Quiz

Tested on three datasets — roughly spherical unequal-variance blobs, anisotropically stretched blobs, and moon-shaped clusters — K-Means only produced sensible clusters on the spherical blobs. It visibly misassigns points on the stretched and non-convex shapes.

  • Sensitive to outliers — they distort centroids and inertia
  • Can converge to local optima
  • Requires similarly-scaled features (see Ch.2 scaling)
  • Not suited to categorical data — squared Euclidean distance is meaningless there
  • Assumes roughly spherical, similarly-sized clusters

Bottom line: simple, fast, and genuinely useful for quick exploratory segmentation — as long as its spherical-cluster assumption isn't badly violated.

At scale

Vanilla K-Means struggles with large data: repeated full-dataset iteration is expensive, memory grows, initialization gets harder, and high dimensionality complicates both computation and interpretation. Scalable variants exist (Mini-Batch K-Means, K-Means++); distributed tooling includes PySpark and RAPIDS.

Evaluating & Interpreting Clusters

Clustering is exploratory — the payoff is in what the groups actually mean.

MetricWhat it captures
Silhouette CoefficientHow similar a point is to its own cluster vs. other clusters
Completeness ScoreWhether all same-true-label samples land in one cluster
Normalized Mutual Information (NMI)Alignment between clusters and true labels
Davies-Bouldin IndexWithin-cluster similarity vs. between-cluster dissimilarity
OthersCalinski-Harabasz, Rand Index, Adjusted Rand Index, Homogeneity Score
Rule

The metric that technically optimizes a clustering isn't necessarily the most useful one to report. Real value comes from profiling each cluster — dominant characteristics, mean/median/mode per feature, which variables actually differentiate the groups — and giving clusters meaningful names ("loyal customers," "support complainers") rather than just cluster IDs.
