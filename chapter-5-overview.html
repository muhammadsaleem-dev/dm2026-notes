Data Mining Bootcamp · Module 5

From Regression to Neural Networks

Zero to hero, from here
The biggest, most math-heavy module — every formula worked by hand

The one idea this whole module builds toward: a neuron is logistic regression, and a network is many of them chained together. Gradient descent, backprop, and a full forward+backward pass computed by hand, start to finish.

By the end of this module, you'll be able to
  • Explain why a neural network is "just" stacked logistic regression, and why depth requires non-linear activations to matter at all
  • Compute MSE and one full gradient-descent update step by hand for a tiny dataset
  • Compute sigmoid, binary cross-entropy, and its gradient by hand for a single data point
  • Forward-propagate and backpropagate through a 2-input, 1-hidden-neuron, 1-output network by hand
  • Explain vanishing/exploding gradients, and choose sensible defaults for network size, activation, and optimizer

1. The Linear Soup Cart

Linear Regression

A chef uses a simple linear recipe. If he adds exactly 2 carrots, a mechanical dial measuring the soup's "flavor" predictably ticks up by exactly 2 units.

2. The Taste Test

Mean Squared Error (MSE)

The chef measures exactly how bad the soup is by squaring the mistakes. Being off by a little is fine, but being off by a lot is a squared disaster!

3. The Diet Spices

Regularization (Ridge & Lasso)

To prevent the soup from becoming too complex (overfitting), the health inspector forces the chef to shrink his spice usage or drop some entirely.

4. Walking Down the Mountain

Gradient Descent

The chef is blindfolded on a foggy, hilly mountain (the cost landscape). He feels the slope with his foot and takes one small step downhill to minimize the cost.

5. The 'Yes/No' Filter

Logistic Regression & Sigmoid

Instead of asking 'how spicy is it?', the restaurant now asks 'is it Spicy: YES or NO?'. A 'Squashing Filter' forces raw flavors into a neat probability between 0 and 1.

6. The Harsh Critic

Binary Cross Entropy

The Head Chef uses a harsh new scorecard. If the apprentice is 99% confident the soup is NOT spicy, but it IS, the Chef punishes him infinitely more than just a small mistake!

7. The Mega-Kitchen

Neural Network Architecture

The single soup cart expands into an industrial kitchen with multiple 'Hidden Layers' (Prep Cooks -> Line Cooks) and a final output window (Head Chef).

8. Hierarchical Cooking

Hierarchical Features

Prep cooks chop vegetables (edges), line cooks assemble them (contours), and the Head Chef plates the final masterpiece (objects). Depth allows the network to learn combinations.

9. The Magic Spices

Non-Linear Activations (ReLU)

The chefs use a magic spice (ReLU) that only activates if the flavor is positive, and zeroes out negative flavors. This sudden, non-linear change allows them to cook complex boundaries!

10. The Assembly Line

The Forward Pass

Raw ingredients rush through the loading dock, get chopped, cooked, spiced, and finally plated by the Head Chef to serve to the customer to get a prediction.

11. The Chain of Blame

Backpropagation

The customer complains! The Head Chef calculates the error, yells at the line cooks, who then yell at the prep cooks, passing the blame backward proportionally based on who added what.

12. The Fading Voice

Vanishing Gradients

The kitchen is so deep that the Head Chef's yells fade away. The prep cooks at the very beginning can't hear the instructions, so their weights never update and they never improve.

13. The Exploding Voice

Exploding Gradients

A small complaint gets amplified backward by middle managers. By the time it hits the ground floor, it explodes like a sonic boom! The prep cooks panic and ruin their dials.

14. The Taste-Tester Batch

Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD)

Instead of cooking the entire menu before adjusting the dials, they taste a small, random 'mini-batch' of soups and adjust immediately. It's noisier, but learns hundreds of times faster.

15. The Heavy Cart

Momentum

The chef puts his spice cart on heavy wheels. As he rolls downhill, he builds physical momentum, rolling right over small bumps (local minima) without getting stuck.

16. The Smart Dials & Convergence

Adam Optimizer

They install highly advanced, adaptive dials on every stove. These dials remember past momentum and automatically adjust their own sensitivity for every single ingredient independently.
